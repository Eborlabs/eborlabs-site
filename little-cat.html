A gentle care diary for little ones.

Seizures, sleep, feeds and medicines, shared between everyone who looks after a baby or young child, especially a child with epilepsy. Whatever one carer logs shows on the others' phones in seconds.

What it does

One diary on every carer's phone, and it works offline

In a seizure

  • One big button starts a seizure timer on every family phone at once, and the others are told who started it.
  • The child's rescue plan is on the timer screen, with an alarm at the plan's alert time and Call 999 always in reach.
  • Count spasms in a cluster, even from two phones, and log rescue medicine.
  • First-aid steps and when to call 999, from the NHS, with links.

Every day

  • One-tap logging of feeds, nappies, sleep, medicines, temperature, symptoms, photos and notes, with a night mode for 3am.
  • Medicine checklist and reminders, with a "who has the child?" duty so only one parent is woken.
  • Growth charts (UK-WHO), vaccinations (UK schedule) and development milestones.

For the doctors

  • A PDF clinic summary, a spreadsheet export and an emergency card with a QR code.
  • A one-page printable seizure guide for other carers.
  • Read-only links for the care team that expire.
  • Seizures per day, and trends with medicine changes marked.

Help when you want it

  • AI help, from Claude by Anthropic, is available as a paid option: reading a clinic letter or a medicine label, summaries, a help chat and voice logging. Email littlecat@eborlabs.com to ask.
  • Roles for a parent, a carer, view-only, and a nursery or childminder, who sees the last 24 hours and the emergency details only.

Careful with advice, and with your data

Health advice from the NHS, with sources

Every piece of health advice in Little Cat comes from nhs.uk, with the source linked underneath it, and is checked each month for NHS changes.

Private: only people you invite

The diary is kept in the UK, in London, encrypted. No adverts, no tracking, and it is never sold. Read Little Cat's privacy policy.

Little Cat is a care diary, not a medical device, and it has not been reviewed by a clinician. It does not diagnose, predict or detect seizures, and it does not replace your child's care plan, their epilepsy team or 999. In an emergency, call 999.
