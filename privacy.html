Two different roles

Data protection law distinguishes between deciding what happens to personal data and simply handling it for somebody else. Ebor Labs does both, in different contexts, and the distinction changes who you should contact.

Where Ebor Labs decides — its own business contacts

If you email an enquiry, ask for a quote, or become a client contact, Ebor Labs is the controller of that information. In practice this is your name, your email address, your employer, and whatever you chose to write.

It is held because there is a legitimate interest in answering you and in keeping a record of business correspondence. It is not sold, not shared for marketing, and not used to profile anyone.

Where a client decides — candidates and their records

When a training provider or employer uses the assessment platform, they decide what is collected and why. They are the controller. Ebor Labs provides and maintains the platform on their instructions, which makes it a processor.

If you sat an assessment and want to see, correct or delete your record, contact the organisation that ran your training. They can do it directly. Ebor Labs will help them, but will not act on their data without them.

What the platform holds about a candidate

  • Name, work email address, and employer
  • Optionally a job title and branch, where the client asks for them
  • Answers given, score, start and finish times, and whether a certificate was issued
  • An authorisation note if extra time was arranged

That last point is deliberately narrow. Where a candidate is granted extra time, the platform records that it was authorised. It does not record why, and it is designed so that a diagnosis or medical detail cannot be stored against a person.

The e-invoicing tools hold nothing at all

The data-readiness tools at einvoicecheck.eborlabs.com run entirely inside your own browser. Files you open there are never sent anywhere, are not uploaded, and are not visible to Ebor Labs. Closing the tab ends it. This is a property of how the tools are built, not a policy that could change quietly.

If you submit an enquiry form on that site, the details you type are sent to Ebor Labs. That is a separate act from using the tools.

How long things are kept

  • Candidate records — for as long as the client requires them, since they usually have their own regulatory retention period. The client decides; Ebor Labs does not delete their records on its own initiative.
  • Business correspondence — while the relationship is live, and for six years afterwards, which is the ordinary limitation period for a contract.
  • Enquiries that go nowhere — deleted within a year.

Nothing on the platform is deleted without two people approving it, and every deletion is recorded.

Cookies and tracking

This website sets no cookies, loads no tracking pixels, and runs no analytics. Nobody is counting your visit.

The assessment platform sets one item in your browser to keep you signed in during a session. It is necessary for the platform to work, expires, and is not used to track anyone across other websites.

Web fonts on this page are served by Google Fonts, which means your browser requests a font file from Google and Google's servers see the request.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you may ask to see the personal data held about you, to have it corrected, to have it deleted where there is no legal reason to keep it, to restrict or object to how it is used, and to receive it in a portable form.

For anything Ebor Labs decides, write to ibrahim@eborlabs.com and you will get an answer within one month. For anything on a client's platform, ask the organisation that trained you — they hold the decision.

If you are unhappy with the response, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk, or by telephone on 0303 123 1113. You are welcome to raise it here first, but you do not have to.

Candidates outside the United Kingdom

Many candidates are in the United Arab Emirates while the platform is run from the United Kingdom. Records are held in London, which means UK data protection law applies to how they are kept. Clients in the UAE remain subject to their own local requirements, and the written agreement between Ebor Labs and each client sets out who is responsible for what.
